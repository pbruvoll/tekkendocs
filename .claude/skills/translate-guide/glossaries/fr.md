# French Tekken glossary

Terminology for translating tekkendocs character guides into French (`fr`). Taken from French
fighting-game sources, not invented. **Append to this file** whenever a run needs a term that is not
here, and record where it came from.

## Approach

The French fighting-game community keeps the competitive jargon in English (launcher, low, mid,
safe, unsafe, juggle, combo, mix-up, frame trap, whiff, counter hit, okizeme, poke, sidestep) and
uses French for the general vocabulary around it (coup, garde, chope, punir, mur, dégâts,
adversaire, accroupi, relevée). Follow that: an over-translated term ("coup ventral" for mid,
"lanceur" for launcher) reads as unnatural to French players. Address the reader with "vous", as
French guides and videos do.

## Kept in English

launcher, poke, jab, string, juggle, combo, counter hit (CH), frame data, frames, timing, hitbox,
hurtbox, backdash, crouch dash, sidestep (verb "sidestepper"), tracking, homing, wall splat,
knockdown (KND), clean hit, mix-up, 50/50, frame trap, whiff (verb "whiffer"), whiff punish,
rushdown, chip, sabaki, parry, reversal, Power Crush, While Standing, Sneak (SNK), knowledge check,
matchup, keep-out, okizeme, fuzzy guard, tech roll, stagger, tailspin, tornado, stance, and all
Heat / Rage names (Heat, Heat Engager, Heat Smash, Heat Burst, Heat Dash, Rage, Rage Art).
high / mid / low stay in English, as nouns too: "un low rapide", "un mid safe".

## Quotes in prose

ASCII `"…"` is parsed as a command link, so never add ASCII quotes around French words; use
« » (with a narrow no-break or normal space inside, as French typography does). When the source
quotes an English word (`"electric"`, `"Keep away"`, `"get-off-me"`), keep it verbatim and put the
French gloss in parentheses after it. Keep every occurrence of a repeated quoted command.

## Character names

Same as English, with no article before a name: "Kazuya a…", "Lili est…".

## Register

"vous", direct coaching voice ("Apprenez le punish au jab", "n'abusez pas de ce coup"). Female
characters take feminine agreement: Lidia, Asuka, Alisa, Azucena, Reina, Nina, Anna, Lili, Jun,
Xiaoyu, Kunimitsu, Miary Zo, Panda ("elle est forte"); male characters masculine.

Who is male, female or unknown is listed in SKILL.md. In French: a male author gets masculine
agreement ("je suis connu"); Panda gets feminine; an author of unknown gender and Leo get wording
without gendered adjectives or past participles ("je joue à Tekken depuis…", "mon expérience").

## Per-character terms

Stance and move names stay in English, as in the other glossaries: KNK/BOK/LTG (Leo), MOR/BAO
(Miary Zo), ZEN/OMEN (Jin), SEN/DEN/LEN (Lars), SZN (Raven), HRM (Leroy), IZU/GEN/MIA (Jun),
HAM/CJM/TOM (Anna), DEW/Feisty Rabbit (Lili), SET/KAT (Kunimitsu), BAD (Armor King), GMH/GMC
(Jack-8), PHX (Clive), RAI/FUJ (Heihachi), MCR (Devil Jin), STC/KNP (Feng), IAI/PRF (Victor),
LNH/FLK/PAB (Steve).

## Research notes (2026-11-02)

French written Tekken material is thin: the community talks in videos, streams and Discord, and the
press mostly writes beginner lists. The sources agree that jargon stays English and confirm chope /
déchoper, punir / punition, garde / garde basse, accroupi, relevée, tortue, mur, "un low", "un mid",
safe / unsafe, sidestepper, launcher. Not confirmed by any source: "bloqué / sur hit" for on block /
on hit, "dos tourné" (BT), "se baisser" (duck), "jab accroupi", "coup pour finir le round". A
French player's review is still the best check.
